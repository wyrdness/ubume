## Overview

Ubume is a tragic yokai - the ghost of a woman who died during childbirth or while pregnant. She appears disheveled and bloodstained, carrying a baby or appearing pregnant, manifesting near rivers, crossroads, or the site of her death.

Ubume encounters passersby and asks them to hold her baby, which becomes progressively heavier until unbearable, or she is seen buying candy for her child with leaves that turn to money. Her maternal instinct persists beyond death, compelling her to seek care for her child.

In some versions, the baby is real and still alive, buried with her; in others, it's a spirit. Ubume is not purely malevolent - she acts from love and desperation. Showing compassion can lay her spirit to rest.

She represents the tragedy of maternal death and the unbreakable bond between mother and child. Similar spirits exist throughout Asia, including the Pontianak of Southeast Asia.

## Common Encounters

### The Baby-Holding Request
Ubume appears and asks passerby to hold her baby. The baby becomes increasingly heavy, sometimes crushing the holder or forcing them to carry it to a temple where the spirit can be laid to rest.

**Outcome**: Varies - compassionate response may lay spirit to rest; cruelty or dropping baby may result in curse.

### The Candy Purchase
Woman (Ubume) comes to shop to buy candy for child, paying with money that later turns to leaves. Shopkeeper may follow her to gravesite and discover baby buried with her, still alive.

**Outcome**: Discovery and rescue of living baby, spirit laid to rest.

### The River Appearance
Ubume appears near river or water, sometimes washing bloodstained clothes, carrying baby.

**Outcome**: Encounter varies based on witness's response.

### Spiritual Significance

## Cultural Context

### Historical Background

In pre-modern Japan, maternal mortality was tragically common. Ubume stories reflect these real fears and losses. The spirit's persistence represents society's recognition of maternal sacrifice and the tragedy of deaths in childbirth.

### Social Commentary

Ubume legends served as reminders of:
- Importance of proper burial rites
- Compassion toward the dead
- Care for orphaned children
